## 141. FEDERAL MEDICAL CENTRE, BAYELSA STATE

**MDA Code:** 521027037

The Federal Medical Centre, Bayelsa State (also known as Federal Medical Centre, Yenagoa), is a federal tertiary healthcare institution located in Yenagoa, Bayelsa State capital, South-South Nigeria. Established under Federal Ministry of Health as part of Federal Medical Centres network nationwide, the Centre provides specialized healthcare services to Bayelsa State and surrounding Niger Delta region. Bayelsa State, created in 1996 from Rivers State, is characterized by riverine communities and difficult terrain requiring accessible tertiary healthcare facilities. The Centre serves oil-producing communities in Niger Delta providing comprehensive specialist medical services to population previously dependent on distant teaching hospitals. The Centre's core mandate is to provide comprehensive tertiary healthcare services, train healthcare professionals and conduct medical research. Specific functions include: (a) to provide specialized medical services across major clinical disciplines including internal medicine, general surgery, pediatrics, obstetrics and gynecology, orthopedics, ophthalmology, ENT and other specialties; (b) to serve as major referral center for secondary healthcare facilities in Bayelsa State and surrounding Niger Delta communities; (c) to operate 24-hour emergency department providing accident, emergency and trauma care; (d) to provide comprehensive diagnostic services including clinical laboratory, medical imaging and specialized investigations; (e) to operate intensive care units, high dependency units and neonatal intensive care units; (f) to provide inpatient services across medical, surgical, pediatric and obstetric wards; (g) to offer specialist outpatient clinics in various medical and surgical specialties; (h) to provide comprehensive maternal and child health services including antenatal care, safe delivery, postnatal care and family planning; (i) to operate theater facilities for surgical procedures; (j) to provide clinical training for medical students through affiliation with medical schools; (k) to conduct residency training programmes for postgraduate medical education; (l) to provide internship training for medical graduates, pharmacists, laboratory scientists, nurses and other health professionals; (m) to offer continuing professional development for healthcare workers; (n) to conduct clinical research and health systems research; (o) to maintain pharmacy services; (p) to provide physiotherapy and rehabilitation services; (q) to establish disease surveillance and infection control systems; (r) to provide public health services including health education; (s) to respond to environmental health challenges specific to Niger Delta region; and (t) to collaborate with state health authorities and oil company health facilities.

## 160. FEDERAL MINISTRY OF AGRICULTURE AND FOOD SECURITY - HQTRS

**MDA Code:** 215001001

The Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security (FMAFS), formerly Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development (FMARD), is a ministry of the Federal Government of Nigeria established in 1966 (as Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Natural Resources), having passed through serial modifications of appellations to suit prevailing contexts of mergers and demergers with related sectors. The Ministry emerged in 1967 alongside creation of 12 states from 4 regions in post-independence Nigeria, with each state establishing own Ministry of Agriculture and Natural Resources. The Ministry was separated from Water Resources in April 2010, and renamed from Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development to Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security to emphasize food security mandate. The Ministry headquarters is located in Abuja with the current minister being Senator Abubakar Kyari (appointed during President Bola Ahmed Tinubu's administration). The Ministry's core mandate is to formulate and implement policies ensuring food security, promoting agricultural development and facilitating agribusiness for increased food production and employment. With Nigeria possessing over 84 million hectares of arable land (only 40% cultivated), population of over 200 million people (Africa's largest market), 230 billion cubic meters of water, and abundant rainfall in over two-thirds of territory, the Ministry works to harness these resources for agricultural transformation. Specific functions include: (a) to ensure food security in crop production, livestock and fisheries for Nigeria's growing population; (b) to formulate and implement national agricultural policies and strategies; (c) to stimulate agricultural employment and create jobs across agricultural value chains; (d) to promote production and supply of raw materials to agro-allied industries; (e) to provide markets for products of industrial sector through agricultural linkages; (f) to generate foreign exchange through agricultural exports; (g) to facilitate rural socio-economic development throughout Nigeria; (h) to manage agricultural sector through multi-stakeholder engagements to develop commodity value chains; (i) to promote agro-industrial development for economic diversification; (j) to empower farmers through extension services, credit facilities and input supply; (k) to facilitate market access for agricultural products domestically and internationally; (l) to promote sustainable agricultural practices and environmental conservation; (m) to supervise and coordinate agricultural research institutes including National Root Crops Research Institute, National Animal Production Research Institute (NAPRI), National Veterinary Research Institute (NVRI), Rubber Research Institute of Nigeria (RRIN), and others; (n) to oversee Federal Colleges of Agriculture, Forestry, Fisheries, Horticulture and other agricultural training institutions; (o) to coordinate agricultural projects supported by donor partners including World Bank, African Development Bank, FAO and bilateral partners; (p) to develop strategic partnerships to stimulate investments driving market-led agricultural transformation; (q) to focus on value chains where Nigeria has comparative advantage; (r) to implement agricultural mechanization programmes including tractor production and distribution; (s) to manage strategic grain reserves and food storage facilities; (t) to coordinate fertilizer production, procurement and distribution; (u) to promote cooperative movements and farmer organizations; (v) to establish and maintain agricultural statistics, planning and policy analysis systems; (w) to facilitate land resources development and soil conservation; and (x) to achieve vision of hunger-free Nigeria through agricultural sector that drives income growth, accelerates food and nutritional security, generates employment and transforms Nigeria into leading player in global food markets.
