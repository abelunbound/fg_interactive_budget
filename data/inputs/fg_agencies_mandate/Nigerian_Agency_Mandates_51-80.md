## 51. DAVID UMAHI FEDERAL UNIVERSITY TEACHING HOSPITAL, UBURU, EBONYI STATE
**MDA Code:** 521026022

David Umahi Federal University Teaching Hospital (DUFUTH), Uburu, is a federal tertiary health institution located in Ohaozara Local Government Area of Ebonyi State. The hospital was established as the teaching hospital component of the David Umahi Federal University of Health Sciences (DUFUHS). Originally established as part of the King David Nweze Umahi University of Medical Sciences by the Ebonyi State Government, the institution was taken over by the Federal Government on September 6, 2022, following an agreement signed between the Federal Ministry of Health and the Ebonyi State Government.

DUFUTH operates as a premier healthcare institution with ultra-modern facilities, cutting-edge equipment, and innovative medical technology. The hospital's vision is to be a world-class tertiary health institution committed to effective and efficient health care delivery, qualitative training, and research. Its mission centers on an irreversible commitment to delivering world-class care with ultra-modern medical facilities, a conducive environment, and passion-driven personnel.

The hospital is mandated to provide clinical training for medical and health sciences students of DUFUHS and other affiliated institutions while rendering specialized healthcare services to patients. DUFUTH has established multiple centres of excellence including oncology, kidney transplant with dialysis centre, liver transplant facilities, and comprehensive cancer management services. The institution operates with state-of-the-art diagnostic equipment and aims to halt medical tourism abroad by providing world-class treatment within Nigeria. The hospital has activated primary healthcare centres across Ebonyi State communities including Umuka, Ugwulangwu, Ngbo, and Nwofe to extend its services to rural populations. Under the leadership of the Chief Medical Director, DUFUTH conducts free medical outreach programmes and has performed hundreds of successful surgeries in various specialized areas.

## 52. DEBT MANAGEMENT OFFICE (DMO)
**MDA Code:** 220002001

The Debt Management Office (DMO) is a Nigerian government agency established on October 4, 2000, tasked with centrally coordinating the management of Nigeria's public debt. The DMO was created following recommendations from the International Monetary Fund's Debt Management Department after a comprehensive assessment of Nigeria's debt situation in 1999. Initially an autonomous unit within the Presidency, the DMO was transferred to the Ministry of Finance in 2001 and upgraded to a full parastatal with the enactment of the Debt Management Office (Establishment, etc.) Act 2003. The Fiscal Responsibility Act 2007 further strengthened its legal framework.

The DMO's vision is to be a public debt management institution of global reference. Its mission is to meet the government's financing needs in a prudent manner that supports economic development while proactively managing risks associated with public debt. The Office operates based on core values embodied in the principle of R-E-C-I-T-E: Respect, Excellence, Commitment, Integrity, Teamwork, and Efficiency.

The DMO's statutory functions under Sections 6 and 7 of the DMO Act 2003 include advising the Federal Government on terms and conditions of loans, restructuring, and refinancing; maintaining a complete and accurate database of all Federal Government borrowings (domestic and external) including contingent liabilities; preparing annual forecasts of debt service and borrowing capacity; efficiently managing Nigeria's domestic and external debt stocks including financial and currency risks; managing relationships with local and international creditors and investors; issuing and managing Federal Government securities; publishing debt data; and annually advising on financing gaps and borrowing requirements. The DMO has achieved significant milestones including reducing Nigeria's external debt stock, increasing external reserves, improving credit ratings, and successfully issuing Eurobonds, Sukuk bonds, and Green Bonds internationally.

## 53. DEFENCE HEADQUARTERS
**MDA Code:** 116002001

The Nigerian Defence Headquarters (DHQ) is the principal headquarters of the Nigerian Armed Forces, situated in the Armed Forces Complex in Abuja, which also houses the Nigerian Army, Air Force, and Naval Headquarters. The DHQ is mandated to drive synergy among the tri-services of the Nigerian Armed Forces and coordinates joint operations. The DHQ was established following the 1979 Nigerian Constitution to enable the Chief of Defence Staff (CDS) carry out his duties. It was abolished by the Buhari regime in 1983 but reestablished and changed to Joint Headquarters (JHQ) in 1985 with the creation of the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff. In September 1990, the nomenclature reverted to Defence Headquarters.

The Armed Forces Act provides the legal framework for the Nigerian Armed Forces, establishing that the Armed Forces shall be charged with the defence of the Federal Republic of Nigeria by land, sea, and air and with such other duties as the National Assembly may prescribe. The President, as Commander-in-Chief, may give directives on the operational use of the Armed Forces, including for maintaining and securing public safety and public order.

The DHQ's responsibilities include deployment, sustenance, and recovery of forces deployed externally or within the country; coordinating tri-service operations; strategic planning and policy implementation; and ensuring that the Nigerian Armed Forces maintains combat readiness on land, sea, and air. The control of the Armed Forces, their joint operations, and training rest with the Chief of Defence Staff, who coordinates the three Services while the Service Chiefs are responsible for day-to-day running of their respective branches. The DHQ nurtures a professional Armed Forces that is people-centric and capable of meeting its constitutional responsibilities in a joint and collaborative environment. The DHQ conducts joint cooperation ventures and strategic seminars to optimize operational capabilities through transformation and innovation.

## 54. DEFENCE INDUSTRIES CORPORATION OF NIGERIA (DICON)
**MDA Code:** 116010001

The Defence Industries Corporation of Nigeria (DICON) is the state-run defence corporation established by an Act of Parliament on August 1, 1964, to provide defence materiel for members of the Armed Forces and other security agencies. A West German manufacturing firm, Fritz Werner, was assigned the task of providing technical expertise and establishing the Ordnance Factory in Kaduna. The original facilities were designed with production capacities of 5,000 BM 59 Rifles, 18,000 SMG 12 units, 12,000,000 rounds of 7.62mm x 51, and 4,000,000 rounds of 9mm x 19 annually.

During the Nigerian Civil War (1967-1970), DICON tripled its production rates, making significant contributions to the war effort. Post-war, DICON diversified into civilian products including rural water supply equipment, industrial spare parts, and furniture. The corporation's mission is to operate ordnance factories for the manufacture and supply of arms and ammunition while using excess capacity to support local industries development. Its vision is to continuously produce and improve arms and ammunition quality to meet Nigeria's defence, security, and foreign policy needs to international standards.

In November 2023, President Bola Ahmed Tinubu signed the DICON Act 2023, which repeals previous legislation and expands the corporation's mandate. The new Act empowers DICON to operate, maintain, and control subsidiaries and ordnance factories; establish the Defence Industry Technology, Research, and Development Institute (DITRDI) for scientific and technological advancement; and foster growth of indigenous defence companies within the Military Industrial Complex. DICON has formed strategic partnerships with local and international defence firms for production of ammunition, drones, MESH systems, armoured vehicles, ballistic vests, and other defence equipment. Notable achievements include the 2019 unveiling of the Ezugwu MRAP armoured vehicle and production of medical equipment during the COVID-19 pandemic.

## 55. DEFENCE INTELLIGENCE AGENCY (DIA)
**MDA Code:** 116012001

The Defence Intelligence Agency (DIA) is Nigeria's primary military intelligence organization responsible for collecting, analyzing, and disseminating intelligence related to defence matters within and beyond the country's borders. The DIA was established in June 1986 through Decree Number 19 (National Security Agencies Act) under President Ibrahim Babangida's military regime, following the dissolution of the National Security Organization (NSO) and restructuring of Nigeria's security services into three separate entities: the State Security Service (SSS) for domestic intelligence, the National Intelligence Agency (NIA) for foreign intelligence, and the DIA for military intelligence.

The DIA operates under the supervision of the Chief of Defence Staff and is headed by the Chief of Defence Intelligence (CDI), typically a major general appointed by the President. The agency's headquarters is located in Asokoro, Abuja. According to the National Security Agencies Act, the DIA's mandate encompasses protection and preservation of all military classified matters concerning Nigeria's security, both within and outside the country; responsibilities affecting defence intelligence of a military nature as determined by the President or Chief of Defence Staff; and providing strategic military intelligence to support national security objectives.

The DIA's core functions include intelligence collection through human intelligence (HUMINT), signals intelligence (SIGINT), imagery intelligence (IMINT), and open-source intelligence (OSINT); analysis and assessment to provide actionable insights for military commanders and policymakers; strategic warning and early detection of emerging security threats including terrorism, insurgency, and transnational crimes; counterintelligence operations to neutralize threats from foreign intelligence agencies and insider threats; and support to military operations including peacekeeping missions, counterinsurgency campaigns, and border security. The DIA collaborates with other intelligence agencies and international partners, and has been particularly active in providing intelligence support for operations against Boko Haram and other security threats.

The Board's mandate includes processing pension applications and documentation for retiring civilian staff; calculating and disbursing retirement benefits in accordance with applicable pension laws and regulations; maintaining accurate records of civilian employees' service years and contributions; liaising with the National Pension Commission and relevant pension fund administrators; addressing grievances and appeals related to pension matters; and ensuring compliance with federal pension policies within the agency. The Board works to ensure that civilian staff who have served the intelligence community receive their entitled benefits upon retirement or disengagement from service.

The establishment of a dedicated civilian pensions board recognizes the unique nature of service within intelligence agencies, where civilian staff play crucial support roles in administration, technical services, and other non-operational functions. The Board ensures these personnel are properly compensated for their years of dedicated service to national security while maintaining the confidentiality requirements inherent to intelligence work.

## 57. DEFENCE INTELLIGENCE SCHOOL
**MDA Code:** 116011001

The Defence Intelligence College (DIC), formerly known as the Defence Intelligence School (DIS), was established in 2001 as the training arm of the Defence Intelligence Agency (DIA) under Decree 19 of 1986 (National Security Agencies Act). At inception, it was located at a temporary site within the DIA Headquarters in Bonny Camp, Lagos. Due to the need for larger space and a more conducive environment, the school was relocated to its present location in Karu, a suburb of the Federal Capital Territory, Abuja, in October 2005.

The objective of the relocation was to reposition the school with a focus on capacity building in support of the DIA and the Armed Forces of Nigeria through the provision of real-time defence intelligence to enhance national security. The nomenclature was changed from Defence Intelligence School to Defence Intelligence College in March 2013, thereby encapsulating the Agency's vision to make the institution a citadel of intelligence and security training in Nigeria.

The College's mandate is to provide security and intelligence training for all categories of DIA staff, personnel of the Nigerian Armed Forces, and other security agencies, to enable them to perform optimally wherever they may be deployed. The vision is to develop well-trained, patriotic, and highly motivated manpower working with cutting-edge technology under effective leadership in collaboration with friendly forces, providing comprehensive and timely defence intelligence in support of national security strategy. The College offers various courses in intelligence analysis, collection techniques, counterintelligence operations, surveillance methodologies, and other specialized intelligence tradecraft to build institutional intelligence capacity across Nigeria's defence and security architecture.

## 58. DEFENCE MISSIONS
**MDA Code:** 116015017

Defence Missions represent Nigeria's military diplomatic presence abroad, operating under the Ministry of Defence to advance Nigeria's defence interests internationally. These missions are established in strategic locations worldwide to facilitate military cooperation, coordinate defence attaché activities, and represent Nigeria's armed forces in bilateral and multilateral defence engagements. Defence missions work closely with Nigerian embassies and high commissions while maintaining their distinct military diplomatic functions.

The mandate of Defence Missions includes representing Nigeria's defence interests in host countries and international organizations; facilitating military-to-military cooperation and defence agreements; coordinating procurement of military equipment and technical assistance; gathering open-source military and strategic intelligence relevant to Nigeria's defence planning; supporting Nigerian military personnel on training attachments, courses, and official duties abroad; participating in international defence forums, conferences, and peacekeeping coordination; and facilitating official visits by Nigerian military officials.

Defence attachés posted to these missions serve as the primary link between the Nigerian Armed Forces and host nation militaries. They engage with defence ministries, military commands, and defence industries in their jurisdictions to advance Nigeria's military modernization objectives and strengthen international defence partnerships. The missions play important roles in Nigeria's participation in regional security arrangements under ECOWAS and the African Union, as well as cooperation with global partners through the United Nations and bilateral defence treaties.

## 60. DEFENCE SPACE ADMINISTRATION (DSA)
**MDA Code:** 116018001

The Defence Space Administration (DSA), formerly known as the Defence Space Agency, was established on October 9, 2014, pursuant to the Nigerian Space Policy and Programmes (NSPP) approved in 2001, which mandated the creation of a Defence Space Command to facilitate and implement defence and security aspects of the National Space Policy. The DSA Act 2016 was enacted by the 8th National Assembly and signed into law on February 3, 2017, formalizing the agency's mandate and operations.

The DSA's mandate is to promote national security and development, support military operations, and improve the Nigerian Armed Forces' cyberspace capabilities. The agency harnesses space products and services to aid the Nigerian military and other security agencies in tackling security challenges, recognizing space as a force multiplier. The DSA's vision is to create space competence capable of producing and utilizing space assets to meet the operational requirements of the Armed Forces and other security agencies.

The DSA operates through several directorates including Policy, Plans and Research; Navigation, Positioning and Timing; Communication Satellites; Launch Services and Space Operations; Earth Observation; and Cyber Security. Key achievements include the production of over 640 topographic maps supporting various security operations, identification of illegal refining sites, enhanced monitoring of military zones, and improved border security. The agency has established a Geospatial Intelligence Fusion and Data Center and works to develop Nigeria's indigenous rocket engineering capacity. The DSA provides secured satellite communication to the Armed Forces and is establishing Earth observation stations in military formations across all geopolitical zones. The agency is headed by the Chief of Defence Space Administration and operates in collaboration with the National Space Research and Development Agency (NASRDA).

## 62. DENTAL TECHNOLOGISTS REGISTRATION BOARD OF NIGERIA
**MDA Code:** 521007001

The Dental Technologists Registration Board of Nigeria was established by the Dental Technologists (Registration, etc.) Act (now CAP D6 LFN 2004) to regulate the profession of dental technology in Nigeria. Dental technology is defined in the Act as the art and science of established laboratory procedures associated with the construction of dental prostheses and restoration according to prescription. The Board operates under the Federal Ministry of Health and is charged with comprehensive regulatory responsibilities for the profession.

The Board's statutory functions include determining what standards of knowledge and skill are to be attained by persons seeking to become members of the profession and improving those standards from time to time as circumstances may permit; securing, in accordance with the Act, the establishment and maintenance of a register of persons registered as members of the profession and the publication of lists of those persons; conducting examinations in the profession and awarding certificates or diplomas to successful candidates; prescribing fees for examinations, registration, and other services; and performing other functions conferred by the Act.

The Board maintains the register of qualified dental technologists and issues registration certificates. It establishes and reviews codes of conduct for professional practice and has established a Dental Technologists Disciplinary Committee to consider cases of professional misconduct referred by the Investigating Panel. The Board approves training institutions and their curricula, conducts accreditation visits, and determines which qualifications (domestic and foreign) are acceptable for registration purposes. The minimum qualifications accepted by the Board are specified in the Third Schedule to the Act, ensuring that only properly trained practitioners are authorized to practice dental technology in Nigeria.

## 64. DIRECTORATE OF STATE SECURITY SERVICE
**MDA Code:** 157002001

The Department of State Services (DSS), formerly known as the State Security Service (SSS) or Directorate of State Security Service, is Nigeria's domestic intelligence agency established in June 1986 through Decree Number 19 (National Security Agencies Act) under President Ibrahim Babangida's military regime. The agency was created following the dissolution of the National Security Organization (NSO), which was restructured into three separate entities: the SSS for domestic intelligence, the National Intelligence Agency (NIA) for foreign intelligence, and the Defence Intelligence Agency (DIA) for military intelligence.

According to the National Security Agencies Act, the State Security Service is charged with responsibility for the prevention and detection of any crime against the internal security of Nigeria; the protection and preservation of all non-military classified matters concerning the internal security of Nigeria; and such other responsibilities affecting internal security within Nigeria as the National Assembly or the President may deem necessary. The principal officer of the agency reports directly to the President of Nigeria.

The DSS operates as Nigeria's primary internal security and counterintelligence agency, conducting intelligence operations to protect the nation from domestic threats including terrorism, espionage, sabotage, and other threats to national security. The agency provides protective intelligence and security for high-ranking government officials, conducts background investigations, monitors threats to public order, and works to prevent activities that could destabilize Nigeria's constitutional government. The DSS also collaborates with other security agencies on matters of overlapping jurisdiction and participates in inter-agency security coordination. The agency has offices across all states of the federation and maintains a significant role in Nigeria's overall national security architecture.

## 67. ECONOMIC AND FINANCIAL CRIMES COMMISSION (EFCC)
**MDA Code:** 111009001

The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) is a Nigerian law enforcement agency established on December 12, 2002, under the EFCC (Establishment) Act 2002 (now CAP E1 LFN 2004), and commenced operational activities on April 13, 2003, under pioneer Executive Chairman Nuhu Ribadu. The Commission was created in response to pressure from the Financial Action Task Force (FATF), which had named Nigeria as one of 23 countries non-cooperative in international efforts to fight money laundering. The Commission is a body corporate with perpetual succession and has its headquarters in Abuja with zonal offices across Nigeria.

The EFCC is mandated to investigate and prosecute all economic and financial crimes including advance fee fraud (419 fraud), money laundering, counterfeiting, illegal fund transfers, futures market fraud, fraudulent encashment of negotiable instruments, computer credit card fraud, contract scams, and other financial crimes. The Commission also enforces the provisions of various laws including the Money Laundering Act, the Failed Banks Act, the Banks and Other Financial Institutions Act, and relevant sections of the Criminal and Penal Codes relating to economic and financial crimes.

The EFCC's functions include investigating reports of financial crimes and prosecuting offenders; identifying, tracing, freezing, confiscating, and seizing proceeds derived from terrorist financing and related offences; adopting measures to eradicate the commission of economic and financial crimes; collaborating with government bodies within and outside Nigeria on the eradication of economic and financial crimes; examining and investigating reported cases of suspicious financial transactions; establishing and maintaining a system for identifying, collecting, analyzing, and disseminating information on economic and financial crimes. The Commission has secured numerous convictions and recovered substantial assets, though it has faced criticism regarding political influence and selective enforcement. The EFCC Academy provides training for investigative officers.

The school is part of the network of federal cooperative colleges supervised by the Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development.

The school's mandate includes providing formal cooperative education through certificate and diploma programmes; training cooperative society officers, managers, and board members; conducting short courses and workshops on cooperative management and governance; developing curricula relevant to modern cooperative practice; conducting research on cooperative development issues; providing consultancy services to cooperative societies and government agencies; promoting cooperative principles and best practices; and supporting the government's cooperative reform and revival initiatives.

The school's programmes cover cooperative economics and management, cooperative law, accounting for cooperatives, cooperative governance, and related business disciplines. Graduates are equipped to serve in cooperative societies, government cooperative departments, agricultural organizations, and financial institutions. The school contributes to the Federal Government's agenda of reviving and reforming cooperative societies as vehicles for economic empowerment, particularly in agriculture. The Sokoto location enables the school to serve the cooperative development needs of the northern region while contributing to national cooperative education objectives.

## 74. FEDERAL CAPITAL TERRITORY ADMINISTRATION (FCTA)
**MDA Code:** 437001001

The Federal Capital Territory Administration (FCTA) is the Nigerian ministry that administers the Federal Capital Territory (FCT), created following the Federal Capital Territory Act of 1976, which established the FCT as Nigeria's new federal capital. The FCTA was established on December 31, 2004, by President Olusegun Obasanjo following the scrapping of the Ministry of the Federal Capital Territory. The FCT is administered in a dual function of a Federal Ministry and a State Government, as Section 299 of the 1999 Constitution provides that the FCT is to be treated as if it were one of the states of the federation.

The FCTA is headed by a Minister appointed by the President, who exercises powers delegated by the President (who acts as the Governor of the FCT). The Permanent Secretary is the Accounting Officer and Chief Administrative Officer. Seven mandate secretariats were created: Education, Transport, Agriculture and Rural Development, Health and Human Services, Social Development, Legal Services, and Area Council Services. The Federal Capital Development Authority (FCDA) oversees physical infrastructure development.

The FCTA's mandate includes building and administering the Federal Capital Territory in compliance with the Abuja Master Plan; providing governance, infrastructure, and services to FCT residents; implementing policies for urban development, housing, and land administration; managing the six area councils (Abuja Municipal, Gwagwalada, Abaji, Kuje, Bwari, and Kwali); ensuring provision of utilities, transportation, and social services; maintaining security, environmental standards, and public health; enforcing building codes and development control; and managing the transition from Lagos to Abuja as Nigeria's capital. The FCTA operates through various departments and agencies to deliver municipal services while maintaining Abuja's status as one of Africa's best-planned cities.

## 75. FEDERAL CHARACTER COMMISSION (FCC)
**MDA Code:** 149001001

The Federal Character Commission (FCC) is an agency of the Federal Republic of Nigeria established by Act No. 34 of 1996 (now CAP F7 LFN 2004) to implement and enforce the Federal Character Principle of fairness and equity in the distribution of public posts and socio-economic infrastructures among the various federating units. The Commission is one of fourteen federal executive bodies established by Section 153 of the 1999 Constitution. The Commission comprises a chairman and representatives of each state of the Federation and the Federal Capital Territory, all appointed by the President subject to Senate confirmation.

The Commission's statutory functions under Section 4 of the FCC Act include working out an equitable formula for the distribution of all cadres of posts in the civil and public services of the Federation and States, the armed forces, the Nigeria Police Force and other security agencies, government-owned bodies corporate, and extra-ministerial departments and parastatals; promoting, monitoring, and enforcing compliance with the principles of proportional sharing of all bureaucratic, economic, media, and political posts at all levels of government; taking legal measures including prosecuting heads or staff of ministries or agencies that fail to comply with federal character principles; advising governments on redressing problems of imbalances and reducing fears of relative deprivation and marginalisation; and ensuring clear criteria and conditions for appointments are established.

The FCC monitors recruitment exercises across federal establishments to ensure equitable distribution among states and the FCT. The Commission categorizes representation into adequately represented (2.5-3%), under-represented (1.5-2.4%), grossly under-represented (under 1.5%), and over-represented (above 3%) status. The Commission's mandate extends to ensuring fairness in the distribution of socio-economic amenities and infrastructure across the federation, addressing historical inequalities, and promoting national unity through equitable access to opportunities.

## 76. FEDERAL CIVIL SERVICE COMMISSION (FCSC)
**MDA Code:** 147001001

The Federal Civil Service Commission of Nigeria (FCSC) is a constitutional body established with authority to make appointments, transfers, and exercise disciplinary control over all Federal Civil Servants. The Commission's origins date to the Central Public Service Board created following the acceptance of the Nigerianisation Commission Report of January 1, 1949. The Federal Public Service Commission was established on April 1, 1954, through Section 174 of the Nigerian Order-in-Council (Constitution) of 1954. Upon independence in 1960, the Public Service Commission was entrenched in Nigeria's Constitution.

The Constitution empowers the FCSC to appoint persons to offices (other than those of Permanent Secretary) in the Federal Civil Service; exercise disciplinary control over persons holding such offices; and promote and transfer such persons within the civil service. No officer can be appointed into the Civil Service without FCSC authorization if they have been convicted of a crime or had previously been dismissed or asked to resign or retire from government service.

The Commission's specific functions include representing at Senior Staff Committee meetings of Ministries; reviewing and approving recommendations on disciplinary cases of senior officers; ratifying promotions of officers to senior positions, including conducting promotion interviews and examinations; hearing appeals on matters of appointment, promotion, and discipline; ensuring compliance with appointment, promotion, and disciplinary guidelines; coordinating with the Office of the Head of the Civil Service of the Federation; and maintaining standards of professionalism and meritocracy in the civil service. The FCSC plays a crucial role in ensuring that Nigeria's federal civil service operates on principles of merit, fairness, and due process, though its jurisdiction has been reduced over time as various agencies and services have been placed under separate boards.

## 77. FEDERAL COOPERATIVE COLLEGE, IBADAN
**MDA Code:** 215029001

The Federal Cooperative College, Ibadan (FCC Ibadan) was established in 1943 as a Cooperative Training School, making it the pioneer institution for cooperative education in Nigeria. The school gained College status in 1957 and began awarding recognized certificates in Cooperative Studies, drawing students from Nigeria and across West Africa including Ghana, Sierra Leone, and The Gambia. On April 1, 1976, the Federal Government took over the college, integrating it into national policy on cooperative education. The college is currently located along FAN Milk Road, Eleyele, Ibadan, and operates under the Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development through the Agricultural Research Council of Nigeria (ARCN).

The college's primary mandate is to train middle-level manpower for the cooperative subsector of the economy at National Diploma (ND) and Higher National Diploma (HND) levels. Beyond its core mandate in Cooperative Economics and Management, the college has evolved into a multidisciplinary institution offering programmes in Accountancy, Business Administration, Banking and Finance, Computer Science, and Home and Rural Economics. The college also runs Agricultural Technology programmes and plans to introduce additional courses including Agribusiness Management and Agricultural Extension.

FCC Ibadan is regulated by the National Board for Technical Education (NBTE), which has accredited its programmes. Graduates of HND programmes are eligible for the National Youth Service Corps (NYSC). The college maintains the Cooperative Innovation and Empowerment Centre to support the Federal Ministry of Agriculture's agenda to revive and reform cooperative societies in Nigeria. The institution offers short courses, workshops, and seminars for officers and board members of cooperative societies, combining formal academic programmes with practical cooperative development support. The college operates with a strike-free academic calendar and emphasizes practical-oriented education preparing students for careers in cooperatives, finance, and business.

## 78. FEDERAL COOPERATIVE COLLEGE, KADUNA
**MDA Code:** 215030001

The Federal Cooperative College, Kaduna was established by administrative pronouncement of the Federal Military Government in 1976 and commenced operations in 1979. The college was established to cater for the growing needs of cooperative education in the northern region of Nigeria, providing manpower for the cooperative subsector of the economy. The college initially took off at a temporary site located at the Educational Resource Centre (E.R.C.) on Gaskiya Road, Zaria, before relocating to its permanent site on Abuja Junction Road, Kaduna.

The college was established as an in-service training institution for cooperative stakeholders and staff of government departments responsible for the supervision of cooperative societies and cooperative development. Initially, trainees were those sponsored by government and cooperative societies. The college started with courses leading to the award of Certificate in Cooperative Studies and later introduced the Diploma in Cooperative Studies to meet increasing demand for higher cooperative education.

The college's programmes have expanded significantly. The National Board for Technical Education (NBTE) accredited the National Diploma programmes with effect from the 2004/2005 Academic Session. The Higher National Diploma (HND) Programme in Cooperative Economics and Management was introduced and accredited in 2008 as a two-year (four-semester) programme. Beyond regular academic programmes, the college runs short courses, workshops, consultancy services, and seminars for officers and board members of cooperative societies and other stakeholders. The college now offers various ND and HND programmes as well as certificate courses designed for skills acquisition and vocational training. The institution aims to equip graduates with transformative educational experiences and prepare them for the labor market and entrepreneurship.

## 79. FEDERAL COOPERATIVE COLLEGE, OJI RIVER
**MDA Code:** 215031001

The Federal Cooperative College, Oji River, located in Enugu State, was established in 1976 as one of three federal cooperative colleges created by administrative pronouncement of the Federal Military Government to provide manpower for the cooperative subsector of the economy. The college is situated at Industrial Layout, off the Enugu-Onitsha Old Road in Oji River, and operates under the Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development through the Agricultural Research Council of Nigeria (ARCN).

The college was initially established as an in-service training institution for cooperative stakeholders and government department staff responsible for cooperative societies supervision and development. It started with courses leading to certificates and diplomas in cooperative studies. A significant milestone was achieved in the 1988/89 academic session when the college introduced National Diploma programmes in Cooperative Economics and Management. Currently, the college has four major departments with NBTE accreditation at both ND and HND levels: Cooperative Economics and Management, Banking and Finance, Business Administration, and Computer Science.

There are ongoing initiatives to expand programmes to include Mass Communication, Agricultural Technology, Home and Resource Economics, Hospitality and Tourism Management, and Science Laboratory Technology. The college also has plans to transform into a Federal Cooperative Polytechnic through National Assembly legislation. FCC Oji River serves as a host for national training workshops for cooperative stakeholders from the South-East and South-South zones, supporting the Federal Government's Renewed Hope Cooperative Reform and Revamp Programme. The curriculum emphasizes self-belief, self-determination, and market readiness, with mandatory practical entrepreneurship training ensuring graduates become potential business owners.

## 80. FEDERAL COLLEGE OF AGRICULTURE, AKURE
**MDA Code:** 215021001

The Federal College of Agriculture, Akure (FECA), formerly known as the School of Agriculture, Akure, was established on January 10, 1957, by the Government of Western Nigeria. It is one of Nigeria's leading agricultural training institutions, mandated to train middle-level manpower in the field of agriculture. The college is located along Ado-Owo Road, opposite Housing Corporation, Akure, Ondo State, and operates under the Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development through the Agricultural Research Council of Nigeria (ARCN).

The college's vision is to be a world-class agricultural training institution making direct and sustainable impacts on gainful youth employment, food security, and improved livelihoods for farmers. Its mission includes integrating available manpower and resources into agricultural sector opportunities; revolutionizing vocational training and short courses; incorporating value chain concepts and ICT into curriculum; and improving community relations through adopted schools and villages programmes.

FECA has offered both National Diploma (ND) and Higher National Diploma (HND) programmes since 1993 in key areas of agriculture including Agricultural Technology, Crop Production, Animal Health and Production, Agricultural and Bio-Environmental Engineering, and other related fields. The college is accredited by the National Board for Technical Education (NBTE) and graduates are eligible for NYSC mobilization. The college maintains crop farms (including cash crops such as cocoa, palm trees, coffee, and rubber), livestock facilities, and demonstration plots for practical training. FECA is a certified master trainer institution in Agribusiness and Value Chain Studies with international partnerships including Bern University of Applied Sciences, Switzerland, and the University of Queensland, Australia. The college regularly conducts vocational training courses, skill development workshops, and capacity-building programmes sponsored by the Federal Ministry of Agriculture for unemployed graduates, farmers, and agricultural stakeholders.
