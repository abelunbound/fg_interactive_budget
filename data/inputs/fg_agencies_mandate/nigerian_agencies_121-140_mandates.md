## 121. FEDERAL COLLEGE OF FRESH WATER FISHERIES TECHNOLOGY - NEW BUSSA

**MDA Code:** 254009001

The Federal College of Freshwater Fisheries Technology, New Bussa, Niger State, was established in January 1978 as Federal Freshwater Fisheries School under the aegis of Kainji Lake Research Institute (KLRI), initially located at temporary site in Shagunu. Following reorganization of research institutes nationwide in 1987, when Kainji Lake Research Institute (a multi-disciplinary research institute) became a single commodity (Fisheries) Institute under the National Institute for Freshwater Fisheries Research (NIFFR), the college's name changed from Federal Freshwater Fisheries School to Federal College of Freshwater Fisheries Technology in line with similar agricultural sector colleges. The college became self-accounting from January 1989, enabling direct project funding and addressing NBTE deficiencies before accreditation visits. Located in New Bussa, Niger State, the college operates under National Institute for Freshwater Fisheries Research (NIFFR) supervision and is accredited by National Board for Technical Education (NBTE), having been granted approval to mount Pre-ND (2008), ND (2010), and HND (2013) programmes in Fisheries Technology. The college's core mandate is to train junior, intermediate and senior fisheries personnel for inland fisheries development through short and long term formal education, and to train and retrain middle-level manpower in aquaculture technology. Specific functions include: (a) to train junior, intermediate and senior fisheries personnel for inland fisheries development through short and long term formal education; (b) to train and retrain middle-level manpower in aquaculture technology; (c) to encourage women participation in fisheries through post-harvest technology training; (d) to train food technicians, technologists and chemical analysts in preservation, processing, packaging, storage and marketing of food commodities and equipment; (e) to train agricultural extension technicians to carry out extension work among rural farmers and fisher folks; (f) to organize agricultural cooperatives and exhibitions, marketing of farm produce, tools, equipment and assisting in agricultural management research; (g) to train agricultural technicians capable of applying modern farming techniques in direct agricultural production; (h) to train caterers capable of producing acceptable local and continental dishes; and (i) to be role model for fisheries, agricultural and allied tertiary institutions developing highly intellectual, skilled, creative and self-employable individuals through modern scientific, technological, research and development methods for socio-economic and political growth toward national development goals.

## 122. FEDERAL COLLEGE OF HORTICULTURE, DADIN-KOWA, GOMBE

**MDA Code:** 215034001

The Federal College of Horticultural Technology, Dadin Kowa, is a government-owned tertiary institution located at Dadin Kowa, Yamaltu Deba Local Government Area of Gombe State, Nigeria. ## 123. FEDERAL COLLEGE OF LAND RESOURCES TECHNOLOGY - OWERRI

**MDA Code:** 215032001

The Federal College of Land Resources Technology, Owerri (FECOLART), was approved by Federal Government in the 1981-1985 4th National Development Plan to address threats of environmental degradation arising principally from soil erosion and flooding in Nigeria. Established in 1980 to train junior and intermediate personnel to participate in checking ubiquitous land degradation problems and ensuring sustainable agricultural production while conserving soil, the college is located at KM4 Old Orlu Road, Egbeada, Owerri, Imo State (temporary site formerly occupied by Conservation Centre for Eastern Zone). Academic activities commenced January 20, 1992, following recommendations of ministerial task force to address initial administrative bottlenecks. The college acquired 100 square hectares at Oforola and Obosima for permanent site, gazetted by Imo State Government in 1998. Operating as a pace-setter institution organized along collegiate system, the college introduced cognate disciplines under supervision of Director, Federal Department of Agricultural Land Resources, Abuja. However, transfer to FEPA/Federal Ministry of Water Resources & Natural Resources in May 1998 created conflict of ownership constituting major setback for infrastructural development. The college's core mandate is to train and develop skilled middle-level manpower in Land Resources Conservation and Environmental Management to reposition agriculture. Specific functions include: (a) to train middle-level manpower in land resources technology, soil conservation and environmental management through National Diploma and Higher National Diploma programmes; (b) to develop and organize short duration courses for relevant serving professionals and private organizations to keep them abreast of modern techniques in land resources management and conservation; (c) to carry out soil advisory services for government and private agencies; (d) to provide education in soil management, soil survey and land evaluation; (e) to train personnel in erosion control, watershed management and land reclamation; (f) to educate students in irrigation and drainage engineering technology; (g) to provide training in agricultural engineering technology for land development; (h) to train personnel in environmental impact assessment and environmental monitoring; (i) to conduct applied research in land degradation control and sustainable land management; (j) to provide consultancy services to individuals, communities, local and state governments in catchment area; and (k) to award National Diploma and Higher National Diploma certificates approved by National Board for Technical Education.

## 126. FEDERAL COLLEGE OF VETERINARY AND MEDICAL LABORATORY TECHNOLOGY, VOM, PLATEAU STATE

**MDA Code:** 265006001

The Federal College of Animal Health and Production Technology (also known as Federal College of Veterinary and Medical Laboratory Technology), Vom, is one of Federal Colleges of Agriculture located within National Veterinary Research Institute (NVRI), Vom, Jos South LGA, Plateau State, at altitude of 1,285 meters above sea level. The college was established in 1941 as Veterinary School, Vom, with ten students, becoming first Higher Institution in West Africa to produce graduate Veterinary Surgeons before University of Ibadan was established in 1948. Initially offering Veterinary Assistants (junior course, senior course) and Veterinary Officers training, the school discontinued Veterinary Officers' course in June 1950 when University College Ibadan assumed higher veterinary training, concentrating thereafter on 2-year Veterinary Assistants' Course. In March 1980, Veterinary School was formally accorded college status and renamed College of Animal Health and Husbandry with mandate to award Ordinary and Higher Diplomas. National Science and Technology Amendment Decree No. 46 of December 30, 1990, gave legal backing to Research Institute to conduct training for technicians and technologists for award of National Diploma (ND), Higher National Diploma (HND) and Certificates. NBTE granted accreditation for ND programmes in Animal Health and Production in 1989 and HND programmes in 1992, with college renamed Federal College of Animal Health and Production Technology in 1991. In 2014, department of Veterinary Science Laboratory Technology (VSLT) was established with options in Veterinary Microbiology, Pathology, Radiology and Anaesthesiology, graduating and inducting 162 maiden graduands of Veterinary Laboratory Technology and Animal Health Technology into Veterinary Council of Nigeria (VCN) as professional para-veterinarians in 2021. The college's core mandate is to train middle-level manpower in Animal Health, Animal Production, Livestock Extension/Management Services, Veterinary Laboratory Technology and Vocational Training who can effectively utilize training to establish and manage livestock farms. Specific functions include: (a) to provide Preliminary National Diploma in Science and Technology (1 year); (b) to offer National Diploma (ND) in Animal Health and Production Technology (2 years); (c) to provide Higher National Diploma (HND) in Animal Health (2 years) with veterinary laboratory technology specialization; (d) to offer Higher National Diploma (HND) in Animal Production (2 years); (e) to provide Higher National Diploma (HND) in Veterinary Science Laboratory Technology with options in Veterinary Microbiology, Pathology, Radiology and Anaesthesiology; (f) to train extension staff capable of bringing agro-veterinary research and innovations to livestock farmers and feeding back farmers' problems to authorities; (g) to produce men and women knowledgeable in animal production who can establish and manage livestock farms to maximum advantage; (h) to train para-veterinarians and veterinary laboratory technologists; (i) to offer degree programmes in Agricultural Science (Animal Production) through affiliation with Abubakar Tafawa Balewa University, Bauchi; (j) to provide Post Graduate Diploma in Animal Health and Production; (k) to offer short courses in animal health, poultry production, beef and dairy production, small ruminants production, and agro-forestry; (l) to produce job-providers not job-seekers; and (m) to award certificates preparing graduates for induction into Veterinary Council of Nigeria and National Youth Service Corps mobilization.

## 128. FEDERAL COMPETITION AND CONSUMER PROTECTION TRIBUNAL

**MDA Code:** 222032001

The Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Tribunal (FCCPT or CCPT) was established by the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act, 2018 (FCCPA), which commenced January 30, 2019, repealing the Consumer Protection Act CAP C25, Laws of the Federation of Nigeria 2004. The FCCPA established two institutions for enforcement: Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (FCCPC) and the Competition and Consumer Protection Tribunal (CCPT). The Tribunal is composed of a Chairman (must be lawyer with 10 years post-qualification experience in competition law, consumer protection law, commercial and industrial law) and six other members with 10 years professional experience in competition and consumer protection law, commerce and industry, public affairs, economics, finance, or business administration, appointed by President subject to Senate confirmation. Members serve five-year tenure from confirmation date or until attaining seventy years, whichever comes first. The Tribunal's core mandate is to adjudicate and exercise jurisdiction, powers and authority conferred by the FCCPA throughout Nigeria for promotion of competition and consumer protection. Specific functions and powers include: (a) to hear and determine appeals or review decisions of the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission throughout the Federation; (b) to adjudicate on matters arising from implementation of the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act 2018; (c) to hear appeals or review decisions of sector-specific authorities after such appeals have first been heard and determined by the Commission; (d) to exercise jurisdiction over all matters relating to competition and consumer protection as conferred by FCCPA; (e) to impose penalties including administrative penalties, fines and sanctions for violations of competition and consumer protection provisions; (f) to order sale of assets where necessary to remedy anti-competitive conduct; (g) to grant injunctions, make cease and desist orders, and issue other equitable remedies; (h) to review and approve or reject Commission decisions on mergers, acquisitions, monopoly investigations and restrictive agreements; (i) to hear complaints directly filed before it by affected parties; (j) to consider matters referred to it by the Commission; (k) to enforce consumer protection rights and remedies; (l) to adjudicate disputes involving anti-competitive agreements, abuse of dominant position, monopoly situations, misleading and deceptive practices, unfair business practices, merger approvals and price regulations; (m) to make procedural rules governing its operations; (n) to maintain public records and make decisions publicly accessible; and (o) to register its decisions at Federal High Court for enforcement purposes. Appeals from Tribunal decisions go to Court of Appeal within 30 days. Under Section 104 FCCPA, the Act's provisions override all other laws (subject to Constitution) in matters relating to competition and consumer protection, establishing Tribunal's precedence in such matters.

## 129. FEDERAL FIRE SERVICE

**MDA Code:** 124007001

The Federal Fire Service (FFS) was established by an Act of Parliament in April 1963, tracing origins to 1901 when Fire Service in Nigeria was established as arm of Lagos Police Fire Brigade. Operating under Fire Service Act (CAP F17 LFN 2004), the Federal Fire Service is under supervision of Ministry of Interior through Civil Defence, Fire, Immigration and Prisons Services Board (CDFIPB) established by Decree No.14 of July 1986. The Federal Government reform of Fire Services in 2007 significantly redefined the Service's mandate beyond traditional firefighting to include regulatory and policy functions. The Federal Fire Service consists of Federal Fire Commissioner (responsible for efficient administration and government of the Service under Minister's general orders and directions) and such other officers and firemen of various ranks appointed by Federal Civil Service Commission. Headquarters located at Mohammadu Buhari Way, Area 10, Garki, Abuja, with twelve zonal commands distributed nationwide. The Fire Service's core mandate encompasses firefighting, fire prevention, rescue operations and regulatory oversight of fire services nationally. Under 2007 reform, specific functions include: (a) to extinguish, control and prevent fire for saving and protection of life and property; (b) to perform humanitarian and rescue works as required under Minister's authority; (c) to regulate State and Private Fire Services throughout Nigeria; (d) to prescribe and monitor standards for promotion of fire education, fire prevention, fire safety, mitigation and investigation; (e) to ensure effective discharge of functions by State Fire Services; (f) to provide policy direction for the Nation's Fire Services; (g) to ensure standardization of fire and rescue equipment manufactured or imported into Nigeria; (h) to encourage establishment by State Fire Services of adequate number of fire stations and fire posts; (i) to establish National Fire Academy and other training facilities for training firefighters within Nigeria and African sub-region; (j) to formulate and develop training curricula and requirements for fire fighters; (k) to provide paramedical and information services during emergencies; (l) to conduct topography of allotted areas and jurisdictions; (m) to effect rescues during fire incidents, accidents and disasters; (n) to collect and collate fire statistics for planning, research and policy formulation; (o) to inspect and ensure fire safety compliance in public buildings and facilities; (p) to provide fire safety education and public enlightenment programmes; (q) to ensure preparedness and impact reduction during emergencies through adequate planning; (r) to maintain Fire Service Reward Fund; (s) to charge fees for services other than extinguishing fires at prescribed rates; (t) to coordinate national emergency responses; and (u) to make fire-fighting and fire prevention services available to non-members through training facilities. Fire Service shall not be used for suppressing civil disturbances. The Service maintains vision to produce firefighters proficient in prevention and mitigation of fire and other emergencies.
